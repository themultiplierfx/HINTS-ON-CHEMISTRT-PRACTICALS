Chapter 8

Identification of Organic Compounds

In qualitative organic analysis, we use characteristic test-tube reactions to identify active functional groups present in unknown sample mixtures. Below is your core laboratory diagnostic chart aligned with the WAEC and SSCE syllabi requirements.

Organic Functional Group Diagnostic Chart

Organic Class Laboratory Reagent & Test Action Expected Observation Result
Alkenes
(Unsaturation)		 Add a few drops of bromine water or acidified potassium tetraoxomanganate(VII) ($KMnO_4$). The reddish-brown color of bromine water (or purple $KMnO_4$) is rapidly decolourized.
Alkanols Add a small piece of metallic sodium to the dry organic liquid sample. Vigorous effervescence; a colourless gas ($H_2$) is liberated which gives a 'pop' sound with a lighted splint.
Alkanoic Acids Add saturated Sodium trioxocarbonate(IV) ($Na_2CO_3$) or $NaHCO_3$ solution. Immediate effervescence; a colourless, odourless gas ($CO_2$) is evolved which turns lime water milky.
Sugars
(Reducing)		 Add Fehling's solution (A + B) or Benedict's solution and boil the mixture gently. A distinct brick-red precipitate forms from the initial blue solution.
Starch Add a few drops of iodine solution to the sample paste. A characteristic intense blue-black colouration develops immediately.
Proteins Perform the Biuret test (add dilute $CuSO_4$ followed by excess $NaOH$). A distinct violet or purple colouration confirms peptide bonds.
🔬 20-Year Exam Note: For sugar analysis, the WAEC syllabus explicitly dictates using **Fehling's or Benedict's solutions only**. For starch, rely strictly on the **iodine test only**. Keep your practical reports directly aligned with these boundaries to protect your raw score parameters.
