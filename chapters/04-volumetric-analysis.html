Chapter 4

Volumetric & Quantitative Analysis

Volumetric analysis (titration) is a quantitative method used to determine the exact concentrations of unknown solutions by reacting them with standard solutions of known concentrations.

Essential Formulas & Mathematics of Moles

To master volumetric calculations, you must lock these fundamental mathematical equations into memory:

1. The Concentration Relationship

Concentration (g/dm³) = Concentration (mol/dm³) × Molar Mass (g/mol)

2. Molar Concentration (Molarity)

Molarity (mol/dm³) = Number of Moles / Volume of Solution (dm³)

3. General Titration Formula

When calculating acid-base neutralization or redox stoichiometry properties, use this master ratio equation:

(C_A × V_A) / (C_B × V_B) = n_A / n_B

Where: C = Concentration (mol/dm³), V = Volume consumed (titre value), n = Mole ratio from balanced chemical equation.

Major Types of Titration Systems

Titration Class Reaction Character Common Indicator Used
Acid-Base Titration A classical neutralization reaction between hydrogen ions ($H^+$) and hydroxide ions ($OH^-$) to form water and a salt. Methyl Orange
Phenolphthalein
Redox Titration Involves simultaneous electron transfer (Oxidation and Reduction) processes between chemical species. Self-indicating ($KMnO_4$) / Starch (for Iodine)
Precipitation (Silver Nitrate) A reaction where ions in solution combine to form an insoluble solid lattice product. Potassium Chromate ($K_2CrO_4$)
🔬 20-Year Exam Presentation Note: Burette readings (both initial and final values) must **always** be recorded to exactly two decimal places (e.g., 24.10cm³, not 24.1cm³).
