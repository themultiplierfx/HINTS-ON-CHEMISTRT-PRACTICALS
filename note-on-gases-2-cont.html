NOTE ON GASES 2 (Continued)

4. Laboratory Preparation of Chlorine Gas (Cl₂)

Chlorine gas is prepared in the laboratory by the oxidation of concentrated Hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$) using solid Manganese(IV) Oxide ($\text{MnO}_2$) or Potassium Tetraoxomanganate(VII) ($\text{KMnO}_4$):

MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + 2H₂O + Cl₂(g)

2KMnO₄ + 16HCl → 2KCl + 2MnCl₂ + 8H₂O + 5Cl₂(g)

NaCl + H₂SO₄ + MnO₂ → Na₂SO₄ + MnCl₂ + H₂O + Cl₂(g)

The reaction of $\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ represents one classic method of generating active $\text{HCl}$ gas inside laboratory parameters before oxidation takes place.

5. Photochemical Decomposition of Chlorine Water

Chlorine reacts reversibly with water to produce oxochlorate(I) acid ($\text{HOCl}$). In the presence of sunlight, this acid decomposes completely to liberate pure oxygen gas:

Cl₂(g) + H₂O → HOCl + HCl(g)

HOCl → HCl + [O]

[O] + [O] → O₂(g)

6. Analytical Hydration Tests for Water Vapour

Water vapour shifts the molecular configuration and color profiles of anhydrous indicator salts:

  • Turns anhydrous copper(II) tetraoxosulphate(VI) from white to blue.
  • Turns anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride from blue to pink.
CuSO₄(s) [white] + 5H₂O(l) → CuSO₄·5H₂O(s) [blue]

CoCl₂(s) [blue] + 6H₂O(l) → CoCl₂·6H₂O(s) [pink]

7. Laboratory Preparation of Ammonia Gas (NH₃)

Ammonia is prepared in the laboratory by heating any ammonium salt with a non-volatile strong base:

2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂ + 2H₂O + 2NH₃(g)
🔬 Practical Evaluation Tip: Calcium hydroxide ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) is strictly preferred over caustic soda ($\text{NaOH}$) when preparing ammonia in the laboratory for two core reasons:
  1. $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ is entirely non-deliquescent (it won't absorb atmospheric moisture to turn into a liquid solution).
  2. It is significantly cheaper and safer to handle during heating configurations.

8. Industrial Manufacturing Protocols

The Haber Process (Ammonia Synthesis)

Industrially or commercially, ammonia gas is synthesized directly from nitrogen and hydrogen under specific equilibrium parameters:

  • Catalyst: Finely divided iron or small pellets of iron.
  • Temperature: About $450^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Pressure: About $200\text{ atm}$.
  • Yield Output Ratio: Approximately $15\%$.

The Contact Process (Sulphur(VI) Oxide Synthesis)

$\text{SO}_3$ is manufactured from the catalytic oxidation of $\text{SO}_2$ and atmospheric oxygen:

  • Catalyst: Vanadium(V) Oxide ($\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$).
  • Temperature: Between $450^\circ\text{C}$ to $500^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Pressure Framework: $1\text{ atm}$.
  • Yield Output Ratio: Approximately $98\%$.
