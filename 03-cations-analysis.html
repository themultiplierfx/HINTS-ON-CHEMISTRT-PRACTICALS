Chapter 3

Cation Identification Matrices

Positive ions that migrate toward the negative electrode (cathode) during electrolysis are classified as cations. Cations are also referred to as metallic ions (including the ammonium ion, NH₄⁺). The core examination syllabus focuses on matching analytical precipitates for eight primary cations: NH₄⁺, Ca²⁺, Pb²⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Al³⁺, and Zn²⁺.

💡 Memory Anchor Note: Think of the cathode and cation as partners moving together. While the cathode is a negative physical electrical terminal, a cation represents a positive chemical ion species.

Precipitate Mapping Scheme (NaOH & Aqueous NH₃)

Testing an unknown salt solution by adding dilute Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH_{(aq)}$) or Aqueous Ammonia ($NH_{3(aq)}$) dropwise, and then in excess, systematically separates and isolates these metal ions based on solubility trends.

Cation Effect of Dilute NaOH(aq) Effect of Aqueous NH₃(aq)
Ammonium
(NH₄⁺)		 No precipitate forms. On heating gently, a gas with a sharp, pungent smell is evolved ($NH_3$) which turns red litmus blue. No observation or reaction occurs.
Calcium
(Ca²⁺)		 White precipitate forms. It remains insoluble when excess NaOH is added. No precipitate or reaction forms.
Lead
(Pb²⁺)		 White precipitate forms. It completely dissolves to a colourless solution in excess NaOH. White precipitate forms. It remains insoluble when excess NH₃ is added.
Zinc
(Zn²⁺)		 White precipitate forms. It completely dissolves to a colourless solution in excess NaOH. White precipitate forms. It completely dissolves to a colourless solution in excess NH₃.
Aluminium
(Al³⁺)		 White precipitate forms. It completely dissolves to a colourless solution in excess NaOH. White precipitate forms. It remains insoluble when excess NH₃ is added.
Copper
(Cu²⁺)		 Pale blue precipitate forms. It remains insoluble in excess NaOH. Pale blue precipitate forms. It dissolves in excess NH₃ to form a deep blue solution.
Iron(II)
(Fe²⁺)		 Dirty-green gelatinous precipitate forms. It remains insoluble in excess. Dirty-green gelatinous precipitate forms. It remains insoluble in excess.
Iron(III)
(Fe³⁺)		 Reddish-brown gelatinous precipitate forms. It remains insoluble in excess. Reddish-brown gelatinous precipitate forms. It remains insoluble in excess.
🔬 Critical Diagnostic Trap: Notice that $Pb^{2+}$, $Zn^{2+}$, and $Al^{3+}$ all give white precipitates that dissolve in excess $NaOH$. To distinguish between them, look closely at the Ammonia ($NH_3$) column: **Only Zinc ($Zn^{2+}$) dissolves in excess Ammonia**, while Lead and Aluminium remain chalky white!
