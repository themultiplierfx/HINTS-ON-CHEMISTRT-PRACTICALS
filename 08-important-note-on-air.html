IMPORTANT NOTE ON AIR

1. Volumetric Constituents of Air

  • Nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$): $78\%$
  • Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$): $21\%$
  • Carbon(IV) Oxide ($\text{CO}_2$): $0.03\%$
  • Water Vapour ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$): Variable parameter
  • Noble Gases: $1\%$

2. Four Proofs That Air is a Mixture

Air is classified strictly as a physical chemical mixture rather than a compound due to these four analytical observations:

  1. It does not have a definitive chemical formula.
  2. Its individual constituents can be separated by physical means or by deploying standard separation techniques.
  3. Its constituents retain their unique individual chemical and physical properties inside the matrix.
  4. If each constituent of air is artificially mixed together in the right proportion, air will form smoothly without any liberation or absorption of heat—showing no evidence of a chemical change.

3. Selective Absorption of Gaseous Fractions

When scrubbing or isolating components of air in a laboratory setup, specific reagents are used to trap targeted molecules:

  • To remove $\text{CO}_2$ from air: Pass the mixture through strong alkalis like $\text{NaOH}$, $\text{KOH}$, or $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$.
  • To remove water vapour: Pass it through drying agents like concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.
  • To remove $\text{O}_2$: Expose it to alkaline pyrogallol, heated Copper turnings, or burning phosphorus.

Once these components are removed, the only remaining gases left are nitrogen and noble gases.

🔬 Critical Practical Marking Point: Nitrogen gas prepared in the laboratory from atmospheric air always weighs slightly more than pure nitrogen gas prepared chemically from compounds. Why? Because atmospheric nitrogen still contains traces of heavy inert noble gases like Argon!

4. Industrial Fractional Distillation of Liquefied Air

Industrially and commercially, pure nitrogen and oxygen are obtained through the fractional distillation of liquefied air based on their distinct boiling point thresholds:

  • Boiling point of Nitrogen: $-196^\circ\text{C}$
  • Boiling point of Oxygen: $-183^\circ\text{C}$

During the fractional distillation process, nitrogen gas distills over first because it has a lower boiling point than oxygen gas.

5. Quick Lab Review Panel

QUESTION 1: Mention 3 separation techniques which rely strictly on the difference in boiling points.
Answer: Evaporation, Simple distillation, and Fractional distillation.

QUESTION 2: Which specific component of a standard distillation plant converts gas vapour back into a liquid distillate?
Answer: The LIEBIG CONDENSER.

QUESTION 3: What is the mechanical function of the Kipp's apparatus in a chemistry laboratory?
Answer: The Kipp's apparatus is used to provide or produce an intermittent supply of any gas whenever it is needed in the laboratory.
